## The beginnings

It was 3 January, I was sitting at the table sipping coffee, when a friend sent me a screenshot confirming his sign-up for the Wrocław Half-marathon. Deciding to sign up took me about 2.5 seconds 😂, and 3 minutes later I sent him a similar screenshot back.

It wasn't my first attempt. A few years ago I'd already signed up for a half marathon, but signing up was as far as it got. I knew that this time I had to take it more seriously and do everything to make it happen.

## Training

I knew I'd need help planning the training. I searched the internet and decided to give the Runna app a go — an app with training plans. And it was a bullseye.

The first few weeks were tough. Especially since I set myself too fast a pace and picked up an injury as early as February. A few weeks off right at the very start 🥲

My leg recovered and I got back to training regularly, 4 sessions a week — 2 x easy, tempo / intervals and a long run. And so the weeks went by, and then the months. It was getting warmer outside, and running was becoming more and more enjoyable.

## First start

May brought the time for my first serious attempt — my half marathon debut, but with no pressure on pace. At least that's what I assumed at first.

I started, it felt great, and my race mode kicked in. I managed to stick to my initial race plan for the first 3 kilometres, and then my legs pulled me forward and, despite the awful heat, I finished my first half marathon under 1:50.

I knew this would only raise the bar for the final start.

## Day 0

Race day arrived — 5 months of preparation for one evening. All day long I was on pins and needles and couldn't wait for the start.

Warm-up, emotions, adrenaline, and at 10 p.m. we were off. 25 thousand people set out to bring Wrocław to a standstill. From the very start I pushed a strong pace around 4:40. The fans and the atmosphere of night running pulled me forward incredibly, and the kilometres kept ticking away fast. Around the 15th kilometre I managed to press on a little more and cruise at a pace of ~4:30 min/km.

When I saw the stadium (where the finish line was), I knew it was almost over and now I just had to seal the deal and I'd soon be able to enjoy the result. I don't really remember the finish itself, I guess the emotions wiped everything — but the time spoke for itself: *1:38:02*.

From here I have to give a huge thank you to Michał, who talked me into this whole thing, and to Kuba, with whom I kept an even pace for almost the entire race.

## What's next

A marathon, why not 🫣 The very next day I signed up for the marathon in Poznań this October. The plan is already in place — now it's just run, run and run.
